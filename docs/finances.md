# Finances

Part of Frescati's CLAUDE.md context. See the root `CLAUDE.md` for the app overview, `docs/members-vs-extras.md` for what a role means and `docs/no-show.md` for the `absent` flag this reads.

- **A season costs money, and two collections carry the whole answer.** `seasons/{id}/dues/{dueId}` is one document per charge, `seasons/{id}/expenses/{expenseId}` is one per thing the group bought, and `/s/[seasonId]/finances` behind the Squad tab reads both. Two kinds of charge and nothing else: a member owes their share of the season's bill, an extra owes a fee for each game they actually played.
- **The fees live on the season document, beside `balance`,** because they are settings an admin types on the same form and an allowlisted map is the pattern that document already uses. `total` is the season's invoice, `perGame` is what an extra pays, `swish` is the number that collects. `feesFor` fills in `DEFAULT_FEES`, so a season created before any of this existed reads as free rather than as broken.
- **The bill is split equally and rounded up.** `entryShare(total, memberCount)`, `Math.ceil`, and the direction is the whole point: 31240 across 18 members is 1735.55 each, so rounding down leaves the group ten kronor short of a pitch it has already booked. Over by eight kronor is a rounding error, short is a phone call. An empty squad is zero rather than a division by zero, since a season nobody has joined has nobody to charge, which is not the same as a bill of nothing.
- **A charge is stored, not derived, and `shared/kit.ts` argues the opposite case for kit and is right about that one.** The difference is that a charge gets _settled_. "Paid" is a stored fact and it is stored against something, so if the charge underneath it is a calculation then an admin marking an extra absent a week later silently deletes a charge somebody has already paid and leaves the payment pointing at nothing, which is exactly the side effect `docs/no-show.md` rules out when it says the flag moves nothing else. Storing it freezes the amount and the role at the moment it was raised, which disposes of the two awkward cases for free: moving the fee in March does not re-price February, and an extra later added to `memberUids` still owes for the games they played as an extra.
- **So the app does the arithmetic and an admin does the writing.** `planDues` works out every charge that ought to exist, `missingDues` subtracts the ones that do, and the screen offers to raise the difference. It is deliberately two presses, one to say how many are missing and one to raise them, because "raise 43 charges" is not a thing to find out you have done. `missingDues` compares **by id and nothing else**, so a charge that exists is left alone whatever it now says, an admin may have waived it, edited it or marked it paid, and none of those are things a sweep should undo.
- **The document id is derived from what the charge is for**, `entry_{uid}` or `game_{gameId}_{uid}`, and that is what makes the button safe to press twice. A random id would let a second press, or two admins pressing at once, charge the whole squad again. Belt and braces in the rules as well: `onlySettles()` allows an update to touch only `status`, `settledAt`, `settledBy`, `note` and `amount`, so a second sweep writing the same id with a fresh `createdAt` is refused rather than quietly resetting a paid charge back to owing.
- **The sweep holds the only one-shot `getDocs` reads in the app,** roughly one query per played game plus one for the book, which is why it sits behind an explicit admin tap instead of a subscription on page load. A few hundred reads once in a while, in exchange for no Cloud Function, no new function-owned field on a hot document and no backfill for existing seasons. `fetchPlayedGameResponses` and `fetchDues` are the reads; `owesForGame` is the test, `role === 'extra'` and confirmed and not absent, both predicates taken from `shared/game.ts` rather than re-tested here.
- **The book it compares against is read too, rather than taken off the screen's own listener, and that is not belt and braces.** An `onSnapshot` raises its first event out of the local cache, and by the time the finances screen opens that cache has already been filled by the season's `subscribeToMyDues`, which asked for one person's charges. So the whole book arrives for a moment as the single charge belonging to the admin reading it, and a sweep counting against that offers to raise a season that is already billed. The second press then recomputes, finds nothing missing and quietly does nothing, which is what the desktop e2e pass caught: it runs against the database the mobile pass left behind, so it is the one run where the book is already full when the screen opens. Anything that turns a count into documents has to be counting what is stored.
- **Only one of the two sides is a pot.** The entry fees exist to pay one invoice, so the question about them is never "how much is there to spend", it is "is the season paid for": `EntryFund` has a `target` and a `short`, floored at zero because a season cannot be more than paid for and rounding the shares up means a full squad usually pays a few kronor over. The extras' money is the money there is to spend, and an expense carries **no field for which pot it came out of**, because a field with one legal value is a field that will eventually hold the other one.
- **The books are the one thing in this app that every signed-in user cannot read.** `isSeasonSquad` in the rules is the union of `memberUids` and `adminUids` off a single `seasonData()`. The README's case for an open group is that an extra has to be able to find a game and put their hand up, and none of that reasoning reaches "who still hasn't paid". An extra falls to the second clause, `resource.data.uid == request.auth.uid`, which standing alone denies a list exactly as it does on `watchers`, so the app asks with `where('uid', '==', them)` and gets only their own. Expenses are readable by anyone signed in, since an expense names a ball rather than a person.
- **`fees` had to go into `seasonShapeOk()`, and that is the change here that could have locked every admin out of every season.** That allowlist is a `keys().hasOnly([...])` against `request.resource.data`, which on an update is the _merged_ document, so the moment one season carried the field every unrelated season edit would have been denied. `feesShapeOk(request.resource.data.get('fees', {}))` joins `balanceShapeOk` at the end of it, and `rules/firestore.test.ts` pins a plain season update with `fees` present for exactly this reason.
- **Both Swish mechanisms carry the same string, and neither one talks to Swish.** `https://app.swish.nu/1/p/sw/?sw=...&amt=...&cur=SEK&msg=...&src=qr` is drawn as the QR code and sits behind the button, and a URL is what lets one string do both jobs: a phone camera offers to open a URL, and a phone with Swish installed opens it in the app, because the host serves an `apple-app-site-association` and an `assetlinks.json` claiming path `*` for `se.bankgirot.swish`. Swish publishes no specification for the query string, so what stands behind it is that people collect real money with it unchanged, a housing association's membership fee, a webshop's checkout, an invoicing app printing it as the QR on a PDF, and that `src=qr` is Swish's own name for a link that arrived by being scanned. `sw` is a Swish alias, digits and only digits: `46701234567` and never `+46701234567`, because a plus is what the app rejects as a link with an incorrect format. `edit` is the one parameter deliberately left out, since it names the fields the payer may still change and omitting it locks all of them; the reference is how an admin works out whose payment landed, and an editable one is a payment nobody can match. A space is `%20` rather than the `+` that `URLSearchParams` writes, because a payee reads the reference off a bank statement by eye. The code is drawn at error correction level H with the app's mark excavated out of the middle, and it takes the width it is given up to 260 rather than a pinned size, because a reference at the 50 character limit is 65 modules across at that level and a pinned 200 pixels would leave three pixels a module.
- **What the QR carried before was `C<payee>;<amount>;<message>;0`, out of the published Swish QR specification, and it was the wrong thing to hand a camera.** It is a line of text rather than a URL, so a camera reads it, finds nothing to open and offers nothing, and the only scanner it ever reached was Swish's own inside the app. The specification is real and the payload was correct; it was the wrong thing to hand somebody standing at the side of a pitch with their camera open. What went before that was `swish://payment?data=<json>`, a 2018 reverse engineering with no first party behind it that the app now rejects outright, and the one custom scheme Swish does document, `swish://paymentrequest?token=...`, needs a Swish Handel agreement and a token from a server.
- **Nothing calls `mpc.getswish.net`**: it is not in the CSP `connect-src`, and a payment screen that waits on a third party is a payment screen that fails at the pitch. **Whether the real Swish app accepts the link is the one thing no test here reaches**, so `/debug` has a panel that draws the real `SwishPay` from the selected season's own number and fees, for a phone to scan, and prints the link and all three forms of the number beside it. It exists because the finances screen shows the QR only to somebody who owes money, so proving the handoff used to mean raising a charge against yourself in real books and leaving it unpaid. The panel writes nothing. Testing it against a seeded season proves nothing either way: the seed collects to `0701234567`, which is a placeholder with no Swish account behind it, and the app rejects a link to a number it cannot resolve exactly as it rejects a malformed one.
- **Swish refuses a payment to your own number, and "the link used to open the app has an incorrect format" is how it says so.** That message is the same one a genuinely malformed link gets, so a working code and a broken one are indistinguishable to whoever is holding the phone, and this cost an evening of rewriting a link that was already correct. Nothing in the app can catch it: `users/{uid}` holds no phone number, so there is nothing to compare `fees.swish` against. It is a line of copy instead, on the finances screen when an admin owes money and on the `/debug` panel's number field, and both are aimed at the same person, since the number a season collects to is usually the admin who typed it and they owe their share like everybody else.
- **Marking a payment is a claim about money, so it is signed.** `settledBy == request.auth.uid` in the rules, and `settlementIsCoherent()` enforces the union in `shared/types.ts`: a charge is either owing and unsigned, or settled and carrying who settled it and when. Without it the document admits a payment nobody recorded taking, and an owing charge still holding the receipt from the time it was paid. Paying through the app ticks nothing off by itself, which the screen says out loud twice.
- **An unpaid charge stops you signing up for another game, and a security rule is what stops you.** The rule on `responses/{uid}` refuses a write landing on `'in'`, on create and on update, from anybody carrying a mark in this season, so the lock holds against a client that has never heard of it. Out is untouched and so is clearing an answer, deliberately: a debt is a reason to keep somebody off the pitch and never a reason to hold them inside a headcount an organiser is booking a pitch against. Nothing else moves. The tabs, the other seasons, the vote, the scoreline, the kit register and `/me` all carry on.
- **A rule cannot ask "does this person owe this season anything", so the answer is written down where one can reach it.** `get()` and `exists()` need a path the rule already holds, and an extra's charges are one document per game they played, which makes the question a query. `onDueWrite` recomputes one player's whole ledger on every write under `dues` and keeps `seasons/{id}/debtors/{uid}` in step with it. Recomputed rather than adjusted by the amount that just moved, for the reason `onResponseWrite` recounts instead of incrementing: a delta applied twice is silently wrong from then on, and retries happen. A write that would not change the numbers is skipped, so a redelivery costs nothing and `updatedAt` goes on meaning the moment the debt last moved rather than the last time the trigger ran.
- **Nothing is left behind at zero, and that is what lets the rule be an `exists()`.** A settled-up player has no document at all rather than one saying nought, so there is no field for a rule to read and then have to trust, and no stale zero to get wrong. One document per person rather than a `debtorUids` array on the season, because the season document is readable by everybody signed in and the books deliberately are not: an array would publish the list of who has not paid to the whole app, which is the one thing the bullet above about `isSeasonSquad` argues the books must not do. The marks are read by the same audience as the book, yourself or the season's squad, and no client may write one.
- **The screens read the charges and never the mark.** `SeasonProvider` subscribes to this player's own dues and derives `debtStanding`, so the notice on the season home and the held In button appear the moment an admin raises a charge instead of a trigger's round trip later. A screen reading the mark would offer a live button for that whole window, for a write the rule was already going to refuse. `debtLock` is derived once in the provider and handed to the three screens that draw a `RespondControl`, because the part worth getting wrong is which arm of `DebtStanding` locks a button, and a ternary per screen is two chances to forget.
- **Losing the dues listener fails open, on purpose.** `duesError` leaves `debt` at `clear` and is kept out of the season context's `error`. The rule is the gate either way, so a dropped subscription costs a refused write and a toast rather than a failure screen over a whole season, and it can never cost a way around the lock. A season that will not draw is a worse answer than a season that does not know what you owe.
- **The one way into an In that never sees the buttons is the notification's own,** so `useRespondIntent` checks the lock itself rather than leaving it to the rule. Those actions were sent before the charge existed and the service worker cannot know, so the tap arrives regardless; refused by the rule it would reach somebody as "couldn't save your answer, try again", which is wrong about what happened and wrong about what to do, and it would report a rule doing its job to Sentry as an application error.
- **An admin can chase what somebody owes, and the request says who, never how much.** `remindDebtors` is a callable, reached from a bell on each owing row in the books and from one button under the list that takes all of them. It reads `debtors` for itself and keeps only the uids it found a mark for, and every figure comes off that mark, so no caller can invent an amount or aim "you owe 4 000 kr" at somebody who owes nothing. Naming people where none of them owes is a `failed-precondition` rather than a cheerful nothing, because it means the screen and the books disagree and the admin is reading a stale list. Pressing the everybody button on a season where nobody owes is not an error. That asked for nothing to happen, and nothing happened.
- **The message says the amount, and says the In is held only when it is held.** `buildDuesPush` leads with the figure, because a chase that opens with the season name is a chase somebody reads half of. Whether it adds the line about not being able to sign up comes from the same split `debtStanding` makes on the client, the season's `adminUids` plus the app admin claim. An admin owes their share and is never locked out by it, so that sentence in front of the person running the season would be a lie sent by their own app. One send per person, so the amount in it is always the reader's own.
- **"Chased 2 days ago" is a claim about the player, so only a chase that landed writes one.** `remindDebtors` stamps `remindedAt` onto the mark after the send, and only if a push or an email got through. A chase that reached no device and no address is reported in the toast instead, so the next admin to open the books is not told the job is done. The stamp is an `update` and never a `set` with a merge, because the other thing an admin does while chasing is mark a payment, and a mark put back between the read and the write would lock a paid-up player out with no charge on the books to explain it. `onDueWrite` writes the mark whole, so it carries `remindedAt` across by hand. It survives a recompute and it dies with the debt, which is right, the next charge somebody picks up should not arrive already chased.
- **A season's receipts are the one thing in this app that is not a Firestore document.** Somebody paid an invoice for a pitch, and every member of the squad needs their own copy of it, because a Swedish employer pays a friskvardsbidrag against a receipt naming what the money went on. The file goes in Cloud Storage at `seasons/{id}/receipts/{receiptId}`, a path `receiptObjectPath` derives from the two ids and nothing else, and `seasons/{id}/receipts/{receiptId}` in Firestore holds what a list has to draw without fetching ten megabytes to draw it: a name, a type, a size and who uploaded it. The document is the index and the object is the bytes, so listing receipts is an `onSnapshot` like every other list on the screen, and only opening one touches the bucket. No stored path, for the reason no due stores its own id: a second copy of a derived fact is a copy that can point at another season's file.
- **The bytes are guarded by a second ruleset, and it decides by reading the season out of Firestore.** `storage.rules` cannot run a query, but it can `firestore.get` a document, and `memberUids` and `adminUids` are both on the season, which is exactly the shape the question needs. So the squad reads a receipt, a season admin writes one, and adding somebody to the roster takes effect on the files at the same moment it takes effect on the books, with nothing to keep in step. It costs one Firestore read per request and a hard dependency on that document: a receipt whose season has been deleted is readable by nobody, which is the right answer, and `onSeasonDeleted` is on its way to delete the object anyway. `rules/storage.test.ts` drives the whole thing against both emulators, because nothing smaller can.
- **`getBlob`, never `getDownloadURL`.** A download URL carries a token that works for whoever holds it, signed in or not, for as long as the object exists, so minting one would quietly undo the rule above. A download here is an authorised fetch: the reader's own ID token, the reader's own App Check token, the rules consulted on every request, and the bytes handed to the browser as a blob that `saveBlob` turns into a file. The price is a CORS entry on the bucket for the app's origin, `storage.cors.json` and step 6 of the README's setup, because Cloud Storage will not serve object bytes to a page on another origin without one. Uploading works regardless, which is the confusing part of getting it wrong: the admin's half looks perfect and every download fails.
- **So the shareable link is a link into the app.** `receiptHref` is `/s/{seasonId}/finances/r/{receiptId}`, a screen of its own, and the copy button in the books puts `window.location.origin` in front of it. Pasted into the group chat it asks whoever opens it to sign in and then answers with the receipt, with "not yours to open", or with "receipt not found", which is precisely the audience the rules already define. A link at the file itself would have been one fewer screen and a permanent hole in the only thing this feature has to get right. The screen is also why the copied URL is worth reading rather than only copying: it is printed in a field beside the button, for a browser that refuses the clipboard.
- **Adding and removing stays with the admins, unlike the kit register beside it.** A ball changes hands at the pitch between two people and has to be recordable by either, but a receipt is going to somebody's payroll department, and a file anybody could swap is a file nobody there should believe. Three types and ten megabytes, checked on the client so that picking a 40 MB photo is a sentence rather than a spinner, and checked again in both rulesets, which is what actually decides. There is no update: everything on the document describes the object beside it, so an edit is either a lie about the bytes or a re-upload, and a re-upload mints a new id. Renaming one is a remove and a re-add.
- **The two halves are written in the order that fails safely.** An upload puts the bytes in first and the document second, so a row in the list always has a file behind it; a failure in between leaves an object nobody can see, which costs a fraction of a cent and is swept up when the season is deleted. A delete goes the other way, object first and document second, and a missing object is not an error, which is what lets a second press finish a delete whose first half already landed. `onSeasonDeleted` runs `deleteFiles` over the season's prefix after the cascade, in its own `try`: a bucket that has not been provisioned is not a reason to leave a season half deleted in Firestore, where the cascade cannot be run again.

- **One trigger and one callable, and the trigger is here because a rule cannot run a query.** Everything else is still the client writing through the rules. `onSeasonDeleted`'s `recursiveDelete` takes all three subcollections, and `onDueWrite` stands aside when a charge disappears with the season it belonged to rather than racing that cascade to write a mark under a season that is already gone. The other privileged thing finances could want is a collected total an extra could see, which needs a function-owned summary document maintained by a trigger, and that is a whole Cloud Function so a guest can see one number. Left out on purpose, one trigger away if it matters. `forgetPlayer.ts` needs nothing new: it reports somebody's outstanding charges and asks for them to be settled or written off rather than deleting them behind your back, and every one of those routes goes through `onDueWrite`, which clears the mark.
