# Scoring and man of the match

Part of Frescati's CLAUDE.md context. See the root `CLAUDE.md` for the app overview; see `docs/ratings.md` for the formula a confirmed result feeds and `docs/teams.md` for the lineup a scoreline is played against.

- **Scores** live at `matches/{order}` where the id is the fixture's place in the running order, so two people scoring the same match write the same document. **No match document means "not played"**, same third state as a response, and for the same reason.
- Anyone holding a response on the game can write a score. Confirming the game (`resultFinalisedAt`) closes it to everyone but a season admin, whose correction triggers a **replay**: `replayRatingsFrom` rewinds the ledger latest-first and replays it in kickoff order. Adjusting only the corrected game would be wrong, because every game after it was rated against the ratings that game produced.
- Ratings apply on an admin's Confirm, or automatically `AUTO_FINALISE_HOURS` after kickoff via `finaliseDueTournaments`.
- **Confirm asks first, and it is the other half of the lock below.** One tap applies ratings to everybody who played, opens the vote and notifies the lineup, and freezes the sheet the ledger was computed against. It also sets the price of every correction from then on, which is a replay. The screen asking before the *undo* and not before the thing that made the undo expensive was backwards, and the button sits directly under the table an admin came to read, which is exactly where a scrolling thumb ends up. The dialog names all three consequences rather than only the ratings, because the notification is the one nobody expects and the one that cannot be taken back.
- **Confirming closes the scoreboard to a tap, not only to most people.** `getScoreAccess` gives the admin who may still correct a score a third state of their own, `locked`, and `ScoreboardLock` is the door out of it: the steppers stay dead until they say they mean it, and the dialog says what moving a score now costs. No *score* is asked about twice anywhere else, deliberately, because every other one is one tap for whoever has a free hand and one tap back when it was wrong. This one replays the ladder from that game forward, and an admin opens a confirmed game to *read* it, the table, the ratings, who was man of the match, far more often than to change it, scrolling a column of steppers with a thumb. It is **not** a permission: the rules are untouched and must stay that way, since a correction is the only way a wrong score is ever put right. And the unlock is state on the screen rather than on the game, it is about this visit, so coming back starts locked again, and there is nothing for a second admin to keep in step.
- **Man of the match is the one thing the ladder cannot work out for itself**, so it is put to the people who were there. Confirming a game opens the vote (`motmVotingUntilMillis` on the game) and notifies the lineup; `closeMotmVoting` counts it `MOTM_VOTING_HOURS` later, writes `tournament/motm`, tells the same people how it went and **deletes the window**, which is what takes the game out of that sweep's single-field query for good, and what shuts the rules. Only players in the lineup may vote, and only for one; voting for yourself is allowed on purpose. **Votes are private to their owner**, admins included, and that is load-bearing rather than tidy: a running count visible while the vote is open turns an early lead into a bandwagon. The totals are published with the decision.
- **The turnout is published while the vote runs; the tally is not.** `onMotmVoteWrite` re-derives `tournament/motmVoters`, uids and nothing else, so the panel can say who has answered and who still needs asking. That is not a hole in the rule above but the other side of it: eight names with no picks attached give nobody a lead to fall in behind, which is the only thing that rule exists to prevent. It is function-written for the same reason `counts` is, that no client can read anybody else's vote to work it out, and `closeMotmVote` **deletes it with the window**, from then on the turnout is the sum of the published counts, and a second copy would be one to keep in step. The one disclosure to accept is at the tail: with a turnout of one, knowing who voted and reading the totals afterwards identifies their pick.
- **An open vote holds a game on the season's home screen.** `groupGames` in `shared/game.ts` splits the calendar into four: the next game, what's coming up, what is still being voted on, and what's played, and a finished game only reaches the last of those once its window is gone. The Played list is collapsed by default, which is where a two-day vote goes to be missed. The window is the whole test, so a game nobody confirmed is archived from the final whistle exactly as before, and one whose votes have been counted drops in on its own with no state to keep in step. It never takes the top card off the next game, and the row it draws links to the team sheet where the vote actually is, the same place the notification lands.
- The bonus reaches a rating **through a replay**, never a poke. `computeGameRatings` reads the decision document back and `applyMotmBonus` adds it, so closing a vote asks for `requestRatingReplay` like a corrected scoreline does, for the same reason, that every game rated afterwards was rated against what this one produced. Anything applied in place would be erased by the next correction. It is **zero-sum**: the pot is funded by everybody who played, winner included, because the displayed 0–100 is mapped from a fixed Elo span and a bonus minted weekly would drift the whole group up it. A tie splits the pot rather than the coin, like `getActualWins`.
- **`MOTM_BONUS_ELO` has a floor, and it is a display point.** The 0–100 is mapped from a fixed Elo span, so five Elo is one point and `toDisplayMovement` rounds, a pot under five is applied correctly to a number nobody can see. That is not a hypothetical: it is what shipped when `K_FACTOR` came down from 50 to 20 and the bonus, defined as a ratio of K, went from 1.25 display points to 0.5. Every test passed, because each is written relative to `MOTM_BONUS_ELO` and the arithmetic was never wrong; the man of the match simply read the same `+4` as the four teammates they had been voted ahead of. `K / 4`, half of winning your evening, is the ratio that keeps it at exactly one point at K=20, and `shared/rating.test.ts` pins the **gap on screen** rather than the constant, so the next retune of K fails there rather than in front of the group.
