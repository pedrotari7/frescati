# Teams

Part of Frescati's CLAUDE.md context. See the root `CLAUDE.md` for the app overview; see `docs/ratings.md` and `docs/scoring-and-motm.md` for what happens to a lineup once the whistle goes.

Games of 8+ split into 2, 3 or 4 teams (`shared/tournament.ts`) and play a generated round robin. Squads differ by at most one player; the on-pitch side size is the smaller of the two squads meeting, with the larger rotating a sub through.

- **The pitch does not grow with the turnout.** `MAX_SIDE` caps a side at five however deep the squads are, so a twelfth player deepens the bench rather than making the game 6v6, which is why **twelve is two teams, not three**: two squads of six playing 5v5 with a sub each, one match for the whole slot, rather than three squads of four. Thirteen is where a third team starts paying for itself. The cap is read in three places for one reason each: `getSideSize` for what a fixture actually plays, `describeSquads` for the badge (it describes the pitch, never the team sheet), and the team-sheet page's `pitchSide`, which a card needs because it is not being read against any one fixture. Below twelve none of them bite: no band produces a squad of more than five.
- **Teams** are picked by the pure, seeded `pickTeams` in `shared/optimizer.ts` and written **only** by the `rebuildTeams` function. Rules reject every client write to the teams doc, an admin reshuffles by bumping `reshuffleCount` on the game, never by writing a lineup.
- Rebuilds are **debounced through Cloud Tasks**: a response write bumps `teamsGeneration`, queues a task carrying it, and the handler drops itself if the generation has moved on. Kept out of `onResponseWrite` deliberately, the optimizer is the one part of the app whose cost grows with turnout. Cloud Tasks has no emulator, so locally `enqueueTeamRebuild` runs `runTeamRebuild` in-process instead. A confirmed game's lineup is never rebuilt, the ledger was computed against it and a replay reads it back.
- **An admin can move people by hand, and doing so pins the lineup.** `setPlayerTeam` is the one door out of an automatic team sheet, for the Tuesday where two of the eleven who said In are still on a bus and somebody's brother has turned up in boots, it moves a player between squads, off the sheet (`teamIndex: null`) or back onto one, all of which are the same write in `shared/lineup.ts`. Still a callable rather than a loosened rule, for the reason the rules file gives: `tournament/teams` is writable by nobody at all, which is what stops a player putting themselves on the side they fancy, and the checks it makes (a squad that can't be emptied, a player who has to be in the pool the optimizer picks from) are ones CEL can't express against a list of maps anyway. What it writes is `edited`, and **its presence is the pin**: from then on `runTeamRebuild` leaves the sheet alone, because the next person to change their mind must not silently undo the evening's planning from a screen nobody is looking at. **Reshuffle is the way back, and it is an app admin's**, that button already means "re-pick these teams", so `onGameWrite` queues its rebuild with `force`. Only app admins are *offered* it, which is narrower than the rule behind it: bumping `reshuffleCount` is a season-admin write like every other field on the game document, and stays one. Re-picking is free, instant and leaves no mark, so a button in front of everybody who runs a season is one that gets pulled again and again until the squads come out the way somebody fancies, and a seeded optimizer exists precisely so that they are nobody's to fancy. The price is that a season admin can pin a lineup and not hand it back; the answer is that they were never reaching for a re-roll in the first place, they wanted one person moved, and `setPlayerTeam` moves that person and signs the sheet with their name. The screens follow the button: the pinned-lineup note names Reshuffle only to somebody who has it. The price of pinning is that the sheet and the pool can drift apart in both directions, so the screen says so rather than hiding it: whoever is In and on no team gets a panel of their own, and whoever is on a squad and has since tapped Out is struck through on it. An automatic lineup needs neither said, because a rebuild is already seconds away.
- **Which squad is team A is an admin's to say, and that is how you start with B v C.** Team A is not a name, it is the first index, and `getFixtures` pairs teams by index, so "team A is still tying their laces, start with the other two" is a question about letters rather than about the fixture list, and `setTeamLetter` answers it by swapping two squads over. One idea instead of two: the bibs, the scoreboard and the table all keep agreeing because they all read the same index. A **swap** rather than an arbitrary permutation, so the second tap is predictable, everything the admin didn't pick stays where it is. **Refused once anything has been scored**, which is stricter than a rebuild needs to be and for a reason `selectPlayedMatches` can't help with: a match document stores the two indices it was played between, so a swap underneath one leaves a scoreline that still looks entirely valid and is now about the wrong people. No real constraint either, by the first score, who kicks off has been decided. It pins the lineup like every other hand edit.
