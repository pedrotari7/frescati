# Ratings

Part of Frescati's CLAUDE.md context. See the root `CLAUDE.md` for the app overview; see `docs/teams.md` for how a lineup is built and `docs/scoring-and-motm.md` for what triggers a rating pass.

- **Ratings** are a global career Elo on `users/{uid}.rating`, shown as 0–100 via a fixed mapping (`shared/rating.ts`). A rating moves on how much of its evening a team _won_ versus how much it was _expected_ to, with a working balancer, the result carries information about you only to the extent it beat the expectation. Unrated players seed at the live average of the season's rated members.
- **"How much you won" is a rate, and that is what makes a three-team game worth the same as a two-team one.** What each match was worth, averaged over the matches you actually played, the same currency `getWinProbability` returns, so the two sides of the comparison subtract directly and the update is zero-sum with no normalisation step. It used to be a finishing place scored against expectation, which was **unbounded in the number of teams**: winning your evening was worth `(teamCount - 1) / 2`, so three teams paid twice what two did and four teams three times, for no footballing reason at all, three teams play more matches, not a longer slot. Winning outright is now half a K whatever the count. Equalising had to move one of the two and the one held still is the smaller, so `K_FACTOR` is unchanged at 20: a settled player who wins their evening moves two points of the displayed 0–100, exactly what a two-team game already paid, and the three-team game came down to meet it.
- **A match is half the result and half the share of the goals, which is how a rating hears the difference between a squeak and a rout.** `getMatchScore` blends `RESULT_WEIGHT` of a plain 1 / 0.5 / 0 with the smoothed share `(for + 1) / (for + against + 2)`. Both halves are symmetric, so the two sides of a match still total exactly 1 and the update stays zero-sum with nothing to normalise. It exists for the **two-team game**, where the whole evening is one match and the rate could only ever read 1, 0.5 or 0, a 14-13 and a 14-0 both paid the maximum this file can pay, for a game that was a coin flip. Half on the result so that **winning still pays on its own**: a win never scores below 0.75, because on goals alone a settled winner of a 14-13 moves less than half a display point and reads `+0`, the same unreadable answer the man-of-the-match bonus shipped with. The `GOAL_SMOOTHING` of one apiece is what makes the first goal of margin worth far more than the fifth, 5-0 to 9-0 is a twentieth of what 0-0 to 1-0 is, so there is nothing there for anybody to chase by running the score up on a squad that is a man down. One rather than a bigger number because the group plays both a single long match into the teens and a card of ten-minute matches finishing 1-0, and a share is the only currency those two have in common. Any draw is exactly 0.5 however it was scored: the goals in a 3-3 say nothing the result did not.
- **A fifth of the swing is where you finished, and it is raw position, the one thing here that is not read against expectation.** The rate reads the goals inside each match but knows nothing of the table's tie-break, so two teams level on wins and draws can still come out level or the wrong way round, and one of them won the thing; a group reading a table where they came 1st and 2nd and gained identically will conclude the rating is broken. So `RATE_WEIGHT` keeps 0.8 for the rate and leaves 0.2 for place. Raw rather than expectation-adjusted **on purpose**: an adjusted ordinal can pay a strong team less for winning than a weak team for coming second, which is exactly the guarantee this term exists to provide. The cost is a fifth of a place's worth of unearned credit to a stacked team, capped at `0.2 × 0.5 × K` and standing against a balancer whose job is to see that no team is stacked. The table's goal difference reaches a rating only through here, it decides who is first, never what first is worth. Goals themselves reach the rate directly, one match at a time and flattened by the smoothing above; the aggregate never does.
- **"The team that wins the table always gains more" is the intent, and it is not quite a theorem.** The table ranks on 3-1-0 while the result half of the rate counts a draw as a half, so the two can disagree: at equal points and equal matches, a team with more wins has the *lower* rate on that half, and the 0.2 ordinal only outweighs it while the gap stays small. It takes a team drawing something like six or more of its matches, level on points and behind on goal difference, which no real evening of ten-minute matches reaches. The tests pin the realistic guarantee rather than claiming a universal one; if the fixture list ever gets long enough for it to bite, the fix is a bigger `RATE_WEIGHT` remainder, not a special case.
- **A season's movement and its games round separately, so they do not add up, and both screens admit it.** A display point is five Elo. `toDisplayMovement` rounds a season's summed Elo once, `RatingMovement` rounds each game on its own, so 13.2 and 8.3 Elo read `+3` and `+2` under a season of `+4`, and a season of `n` games can be `n / 2` out. The season number is the one derived from the real total. Working in display space throughout (`toDisplayRating(after) - toDisplayRating(before)`) telescopes and always sums, and was **rejected**: a squad shares one Elo delta at different ratings, so teammates who moved identically would read `+2` and `+3` on the same team card, which is the worse thing to have to explain. The footnotes on the season table and the profile's game list carry the answer instead.
- **Changing the formula reaches history through a replay, and `replay-ratings` is the only way to ask for one from outside a trigger.** Every other replay starts from a game, a corrected scoreline, a counted vote, a deleted game, and a retune starts from *no* game: nothing threw, no trigger will ever fire, and every entry in the ledger is now rated by code that is gone. The script says "from the beginning" and nothing else, going through `requestRatingReplay` rather than `replayRatingsFrom` for the reason that module gives: the rewind and the forward pass are one operation and a script is no more entitled to skip the ladder lock than a trigger is. `rate-replay-report` is how to price a change before shipping it and how to check afterwards that it landed. **Deploy first, then replay.** The script runs `finalise` in-process off the working tree, so the ladder it writes is the *local* formula whatever is deployed, which is exactly the trap: replay before deploying and the next confirmation or correction re-rates its game with the old code still in production, unpicking the new ladder one game at a time and leaving a history rated two different ways.
- A **starting rating** is an app admin's estimate of somebody who hasn't played yet, set from `/admin/ratings` through the `setStartingRating` callable. It stores a real rating on `games: 0`, so `hasPlayed` rather than the presence of the field is what "the ladder has had its say" means, the balancer uses it from their first game, the all-time ladder leaves them off, and the provisional K-factor still moves them fast. **Only settable before their first rated game**, and not by taste: every ledger entry records what each player carried into that game, so overwriting an earned rating would be silently undone by the next replay. Fix an earned rating by fixing the scores behind it.
- **A player's record** (`/u/[uid]`) is aggregated from the ledger and nothing else, in `shared/player.ts`. Appearances, wins, runs, who they play with and the rating line all come off the same entries the table reads, so a correction that replays the ladder fixes the profile in the same pass and nothing can drift. It says how a team _placed_, never "2nd of 4": with a tie for last place the number of teams isn't recoverable from `positions`. The query filters on `positions.{uid}` and rides Firestore's automatic map-subfield indexes: no composite index, no mirrored `uids` array, and **no single-field exemption on `positions`**, which would silently break it.
- **`positions` says where a team finished; `teams` says who was on it.** They look interchangeable and are not: `positions` is a competition ranking shared on a tie, so a two-team game that ends level puts the whole lineup on 0 and every player looks like everybody's teammate. `getPlayerLinks` needs the team index, which is why `computeGameRatings` writes `teams` onto the entry, denormalised for the same reason the rest of it is, so a career screen reads one collection rather than one team sheet per game. It is **optional**, like `motm?`: entries rated before the field existed have none, `getPlayerLinks` **skips** those rather than guessing, and the panel says how many games it actually read so a thin answer doesn't look like a complete one. `backfill-ledger-teams` fills them in from the team sheet, safe years later precisely because a confirmed game's lineup is never rebuilt, and reports rather than repairs when a sheet is missing or names a different squad than the entry rated.
- **`before: null` says a player carried no rating in; `seedElo` says what the game rated them off anyway.** Those are different facts and one field cannot hold both. `before` exists so a rewind can restore *nothing*, which is what an unrated player had, and it has to keep meaning exactly that, but the game did not treat them as nothing, it seeded them at the live average of the season's rated members and moved them from there. Without the seed on the entry, every screen aggregating the ledger has to score a first appearance as **no movement at all**, so a newcomer sat on 0 in the season table while the ten people they played with showed the same ninety minutes as ±2. One number per entry, because it is one number: everybody unrated in a game seeds at the same average. **Optional, like `teams?`**: left off entirely when nobody was unrated, since there was no seed to record; absent on entries written before it existed, where `getSeasonTable` and `getPlayerGames` fall back to counting nothing because the distance genuinely isn't recoverable. `backfill-ledger-seed` fills those in from `tournament/result.changes[].before`, which is the seed the game actually used, written by the same call that wrote the entry, and skips a game whose result is gone rather than guessing. A replay writes it too, so a corrected scoreline fixes historic entries in the same pass.
- **`rate` and `expected` are on the entry because the ledger stopped being able to explain itself.** Everything else denormalised onto it is there so a screen reads one collection; these two are there for a blunter reason. Under the old formula `positions` plus `before` plus K reconstructed any delta exactly, the finishing place *was* the input. It no longer is: the input is a rate over the matches that were played, and those live in `games/{id}/matches`, a subcollection a replay-audit deliberately never opens. Without these an entry records a movement with nothing in the same document that accounts for it. **Optional like `teams?` and `seedElo?`**, for the same reason and with the same rule, entries rated before the fields existed have none, and a reader falls back rather than guessing. No backfill script, unlike those two, because there is nothing to recover them *from* short of re-deriving them, which is what a replay does anyway: `replayRatingsFrom` rewrites `tournament/result` and the ledger entry together, so one pass fills every entry it touches.
